Who this is for

Qualified nurses who want to work in the UK.

That is the whole audience, and we have kept it that way deliberately. Everything here is written for nurses — the NMC route, the exams, what UK wards are actually like, and what employers look for.

You might have a GNM or a BSc. You might be a year out of college or eight years into a busy ICU. You might have passed your CBT already, or you might still be deciding whether any of this is realistic.

All of that is fine. We would rather know you early than find you late.

What we are not

We are not a job board, an immigration consultancy or a visa service. We publish what we know about the route, and we introduce nurses to UK employers who have live roles. That is the whole model.

The route

Understand the path before you take it.

Getting to a UK ward is not one step. It is six, over a year or two, and they mostly have to happen in order.

  1. English test

    IELTS or OET. The one step entirely in your hands, and the one that takes longest to redo — which is why it comes first.

  2. NMC application and CBT

    Your account, your evidence, and the computer-based test. Booking is quick; the revision is where the months go.

  3. Test of Competence invitation

    The NMC reviews what you have sent and invites you to the practical part of the Test of Competence.

  4. A role and a Certificate of Sponsorship

    A UK employer offers you a job and issues your Certificate of Sponsorship. This is the step the Register exists for.

  5. Visa and travel

    Your visa application, then arrival. Your employer handles the sponsorship side of it.

  6. OSCE, then your PIN

    Sat in the UK once you are here. Pass it and the NMC issues your PIN.

Registration rules are set by the NMC and immigration rules by the Home Office, and both change. We explain how the route generally works and point you at the official source. We are not that authority.
