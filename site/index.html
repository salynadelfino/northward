Who this is for

Northward is for qualified healthcare professionals in India who are considering a career in the UK — nurses, doctors, and allied health professionals such as physiotherapists, radiographers and biomedical scientists.

Some people find us with a passed CBT and an OET certificate already in hand. Others are in their final year and have only just started asking questions. Both belong here. You do not need to be ready to move to be useful to us, or us to you.

  • Nurses
  • Doctors
  • Physiotherapists
  • Radiographers
  • Biomedical scientists
  • Operating department practitioners
  • Occupational therapists
  • Pharmacists

Eligibility depends on your profession, your qualification and the UK rules in force at the time. The pathway page sets out how that generally works and where to check it.

The pathway

Understand the path before you take it.

Moving your career to the UK is not one step. It is several, over months, and they mostly have to happen in order. Here is roughly how it goes.

  1. Check your eligibility

    Your profession, your qualification and your experience decide which UK routes are open to you, and the rules change. This is the first thing worth establishing, because it determines everything after it.

  2. Understand registration requirements

    Most UK healthcare roles require registration with a UK regulator — the NMC for nurses, the GMC for doctors, the HCPC for many allied health professions. Each has its own process and its own timescales.

  3. Prepare your documents

    Transcripts, registration certificates, experience letters, identity documents. University offices in India are not quick, so this is worth starting long before anyone asks you for them.

  4. Meet the English requirement

    There are usually two separate English requirements — one from your regulator, one for the visa — and they are not the same. Check both against the official sources rather than what you read in a group chat.

  5. Sit any required exams

    Depending on your profession this may be a computer-based test, a written assessment, or a clinical examination sat in the UK — usually after you have a job and a visa, which surprises people.

  6. Get job-ready

    A UK-format CV, a clear account of your clinical experience, and some idea of how UK interviews run. This is the part candidates most often skip and most often regret.

  7. Explore relevant opportunities

    Roles that match your profession, your experience and your stage. This is where the Northward Register does its work.

  8. Understand sponsorship and the visa

    Sponsorship comes from a licensed UK employer, and not every role carries it. The requirements are set by the Home Office and they change. Check the current position before you plan around it.

  9. Prepare for the move

    Money, accommodation, travel, and the first few weeks after you land. Worth thinking about earlier than most people do.

Registration and immigration rules are set by the regulators and the Home Office, and they change. We explain how the process generally works and point you at the official source for anything current. We are not that authority.
