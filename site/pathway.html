Your pathway

Understand the path before you take it.

Eight stages, roughly in this order, usually over a year or two. Here is how it generally works — and where to check the parts that change.

  1. English test

    IELTS or OET, and there are two separate standards to satisfy — one set by the NMC for registration, one set by the Home Office for the visa. They are assessed differently, and confusing them is the most common and most expensive mistake nurses make. Check both at source before you book anything.

    Official source: NMC — English language requirements · GOV.UK — approved tests

  2. NMC application and CBT

    You create an NMC account, submit your evidence, and sit the computer-based test — part one of the Test of Competence, taken in India. Booking is quick. The revision is where the months actually go, and people underestimate part B.

    Official source: NMC — trained outside the UK

  3. Documents and verification

    Transcripts, your degree and registration certificates, experience and conduct letters, identity documents — often certified, and sometimes verified directly with your council or university. Indian college offices move at their own pace. Start this long before anyone asks you for it.

  4. Test of Competence invitation

    The NMC reviews your application and invites you to the practical part of the Test of Competence. How long this takes varies, and it is largely out of your hands.

  5. A role and a Certificate of Sponsorship

    A UK employer offers you a job and issues your Certificate of Sponsorship. This is the step the Northward Register exists for: rather than applying into a void, you tell us your profile once and hear from us when a live role suits it.

  6. Visa and travel

    Your visa application, then arrival. The sponsorship side sits with your employer. Requirements are set by the Home Office and change with some regularity, so plan around the current published position.

    Official source: GOV.UK — Health and Care Worker visa

  7. OSCE

    Sat in the UK, at an approved test centre, after you arrive. This is the step nurses most often underestimate — it is a practical examination and there are a limited number of centres. Your employer will usually support your preparation.

    Official source: NMC — Test of Competence

  8. Your PIN, and your first ward

    Pass the OSCE and the NMC issues your PIN. You are a registered nurse in the UK. Then comes the part nobody prepares you for: the first few weeks, the paperwork, and learning how a UK ward actually runs.

Where this stops and the authorities start

Registration rules belong to the NMC. Immigration rules belong to the Home Office. Both change, sometimes at short notice, and neither of them is us.

What we can do is explain how the route generally works, tell you which official source governs each part, and say plainly when the answer is “it depends” or “we don’t know yet”. What we cannot do is advise you on your individual immigration position — that is regulated work in the UK, and it belongs with an adviser registered with the Immigration Advice Authority.
