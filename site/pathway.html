Your pathway

Understand the path before you take it.

Moving your career to the UK is not one step. It is several, over months, and they mostly have to happen in order. Here is roughly how it goes, and where to check the parts that change.

  1. Check your eligibility

    Your profession, your qualification and your years of experience decide which UK routes are open to you. Most UK healthcare roles that can be sponsored sit at graduate level or above, and the threshold has moved in recent years. Establish this first, because everything after it depends on the answer.

    Official source: GOV.UK — Skilled Worker visa

  2. Understand your registration requirements

    Almost every clinical role in the UK requires registration with a UK regulator before you can practise. Which one depends on your profession, and each runs its own process with its own evidence, fees and waiting times. Read your regulator’s own pages rather than a summary of them.

    Official source: NMC (nurses and midwives) · GMC (doctors) · HCPC (many allied health professions)

  3. Prepare your documents

    Transcripts, degree and registration certificates, experience and conduct letters, identity documents — often certified, sometimes verified directly with the issuing body. University and council offices in India move at their own pace, so start this early. It is the cheapest step and the one that most often causes the longest delay.

  4. Meet the English requirement

    There are usually two separate English requirements, and confusing them is one of the most common and most expensive mistakes people make. Your regulator sets one standard for registration. The Home Office sets another for the visa, assessed per component rather than on an overall score. Check both, at source, before you book a test.

    Official source: GOV.UK — approved English tests

  5. Sit any required exams

    Depending on your profession this may mean a computer-based test, a written or practical assessment, or a clinical examination sat in the UK. Some are taken at home, some are not, and the ones sat in the UK usually come after you have a job and a visa — which surprises people. Your regulator’s pages set out what applies to you.

  6. Get job-ready

    A CV in UK format, a clear account of your clinical experience, and some understanding of how UK interviews are run and what they are assessing. This is the step candidates most often skip. It is also the one that most often decides whether a strong application becomes an offer.

  7. Explore relevant opportunities

    Roles that match your profession, your experience and where you actually are in the process. This is what the Northward Register is for: rather than applying into a void, you tell us your profile once and hear from us when something fits it.

  8. Understand sponsorship and the visa

    Sponsorship is issued by a licensed UK employer, and not every employer holds a licence or every role carries sponsorship. The visa requirements, fees and salary thresholds are set by the Home Office and change with some regularity. Plan around the current published position, not around what was true last year.

    Official source: GOV.UK — Health and Care Worker visa

  9. Prepare for the move

    Money for the months before your first UK salary, somewhere to live, travel, and the practical first fortnight — a National Insurance number, a bank account, registering with a GP. Worth thinking about well before you have a start date.

Where this stops and the authorities start

Registration rules are set by your regulator. Immigration rules are set by the Home Office. Both change, sometimes at short notice, and neither of them is us.

What we can do is explain how the process generally works, tell you which official source governs each part, and be honest when the answer is “it depends” or “we don’t know yet”. What we cannot do is advise you on your individual immigration position — that is regulated work in the UK, and it belongs with an adviser registered with the Immigration Advice Authority.
