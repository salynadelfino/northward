The UK nursing journey

A practical guide to the steps involved in building your nursing career in the UK.

There’s a lot to figure out when you’re considering working abroad. English tests, NMC registration, exams, finding an employer, sponsorship and eventually making the move.

The process can feel complicated — especially when you’re trying to work out what comes first, what you need to do now, and what can wait.

This is a simple overview of the journey, so you can understand the bigger picture and plan your next step with more confidence.

Before you start

Everyone’s journey looks a little different

There isn’t one fixed timeline for moving to the UK as a nurse. Some nurses complete the process relatively quickly. Others take longer because of work, family commitments, exams, documents or simply because the right opportunity hasn’t come along yet.

Taking longer doesn’t mean you’re behind.

What matters is understanding the general sequence, so you can focus on what is relevant to you now rather than trying to do everything at once.

Not sure where to start?

Start with the things that are within your control. For many internationally trained nurses, that means understanding the English-language requirements and the NMC registration process early. These are important parts of the journey and can take time, particularly if you need to retake an exam or provide additional documentation.

From there, your route may involve eligibility checks, registration requirements, finding a suitable UK employer, sponsorship and preparing for your move.

The important thing is knowing what comes next — and what doesn’t need your attention yet.

Stage by stage

The eight stages

Open any stage to see what it involves, and where the official guidance sits.

01English test

IELTS or OET, and there are two separate standards to satisfy — one set by the NMC for registration, one set by the Home Office for the visa. They are assessed differently, and confusing them is the most common and most expensive mistake nurses make. Check both at source before you book anything.

Official source: NMC — English language requirements · GOV.UK — approved tests
02NMC application and CBT

You create an NMC account, submit your evidence, and sit the computer-based test — part one of the Test of Competence, taken in India. Booking is quick. The revision is where the months actually go, and people underestimate part B.

Official source: NMC — trained outside the UK
03Documents and verification

Transcripts, your degree and registration certificates, experience and conduct letters, identity documents — often certified, and sometimes verified directly with your council or university. College offices move at their own pace. Start this long before anyone asks you for it.

04Test of Competence invitation

The NMC reviews your application and invites you to the practical part of the Test of Competence. How long this takes varies, and it is largely out of your hands.

05A role and a Certificate of Sponsorship

A UK employer offers you a job and issues your Certificate of Sponsorship. This is the stage the Register exists for: rather than applying into a void, you tell us your profile once and hear from us when a live role suits it.

06Visa and travel

Your visa application, then arrival. The sponsorship side sits with your employer. Requirements are set by the Home Office and change with some regularity, so plan around the current published position.

Official source: GOV.UK — Health and Care Worker visa
07OSCE

Sat in the UK, at an approved test centre, after you arrive. This is the stage nurses most often underestimate — it is a practical examination and there are a limited number of centres. Most employers support your preparation for it.

Official source: NMC — Test of Competence
08Your PIN, and your first job

Pass the OSCE and the NMC issues your PIN. You are a registered nurse in the UK. Then comes the part nobody really prepares you for: the first few weeks, the paperwork, and learning how your new workplace actually runs.

Official guidance

Where this guide stops — and official guidance begins

The rules around UK nursing registration and immigration can change, and your circumstances may affect which requirements apply to you.

Nursing registration is governed by the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC). Immigration rules are governed by the UK Home Office. We explain the journey as clearly as we can and link you to the official sources responsible for each stage.

Where the answer depends on your individual circumstances, we’ll say so.

Northward Care is not a regulated immigration adviser, and this page is general information rather than individual immigration or legal advice.
