About Northward

What exactly does Northward do?

Two things. We publish free, plain-English guidance on the UK nursing route — the NMC steps, the English requirement, the exams and the realistic timelines. And we keep a register of internationally educated nurses, so that when a UK employer we work with is recruiting, we can introduce nurses whose experience and stage fit the role.

Do you guarantee a job or a visa?

No, and be careful of anyone who does. We work with UK employers who hire internationally educated nurses, and nurses on our register are the first to hear when a role opens. That is access, not a promise.

Which employers do you work with?

Healthcare providers recruiting internationally educated nurses across England, from independent care groups to specialist providers. We name an employer to you when we are introducing you to a specific role, not before.

How soon will you find me a role?

We cannot say, and we would rather be honest than encouraging. It depends on your stage, your experience and what our employers are recruiting for at the time. Some nurses hear from us within weeks; others wait months. You stay on the register either way, and you hear from us monthly whether or not there is a role.

Cost

Do you charge nurses anything?

No — not for the guidebook, not for registration, not at any point. It is also unlawful in the UK to charge someone a fee for finding them work, so treat any request for one as a warning sign.

What does the route itself cost?

Fees are payable to the NMC, your English test provider and UK Visas and Immigration, and they change. The guidebook itemises every one of them in rupees and pounds, including the ones people find out about late.

The route

How long does the whole route take?

Realistically eighteen months to two years. The six steps are set out here, with a realistic duration on each. Much of that time is waiting, which is time you can spend working and saving.

What English level do I need?

There are two separate requirements and they are easy to confuse. The NMC sets its own English standard for registration. Separately, since 8 January 2026, a Skilled Worker visa application requires CEFR B2 in all four components — reading, writing, speaking and listening. It was B1 before that date, which has caught out a lot of nurses who planned around the old bar. The guidebook explains both.

Where do I sit the OSCE?

In the UK, at an approved test centre, after you have a job and a visa. It is the step nurses most often underestimate, and there is a fee to resit it. The guidebook covers what is assessed and where.

Can I come as a care worker instead?

Not from overseas. Recruitment into care worker and senior care worker roles closed to overseas applicants on 22 July 2025, and the Skilled Worker skill threshold rose to degree level. Registered nursing remains open, which is why Northward is about nursing and nothing else.

Can I bring my family?

Dependants are permitted on the Health and Care Worker route. The detail changes, so we link the authority rather than paraphrase it: GOV.UK — Health and Care Worker visa.

Your details

What is the difference between downloading the guidebook and joining the register?

The guidebook asks for four details and puts you on our mailing list — nothing more. The register asks about your qualification, your English and your NMC progress, because we cannot match you to a role without knowing those things. Downloading the guidebook does not put you on the register.

What do you do with my details?

We hold them so we can tell you about matching roles, and we share them with a UK employer only once you have told us you want to be introduced to that specific role. We do not sell them and we do not pass them to anyone else.

How do I come off the register?

Reply to any email from us, or write to hello@northward.co. We remove your details the same day.
